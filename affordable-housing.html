Briefing Note · CAnD3

Affordable Housing: Solutions for Equitable Access in Ontario

Beren Crim Sabuncu, Jiawen Li, Tuğce Kırmacı Kamber, and Valeriia Yakushko

Download Full Briefing Note (PDF)

Summary of Key Points

  • Ontario is experiencing a severe housing crisis characterized by a shortage of affordable housing, a large supply gap, and rapidly rising rents.
  • These challenges disproportionately affect vulnerable groups, including newcomers, single mothers, and Indigenous peoples.
  • Current policies are relatively strong at the macro level but insufficiently address mid-range affordability, discrimination in housing markets, and differences across housing types, locations, and tenure.
  • The note proposes three policy directions: (1) expanding portable housing benefits for equity-priority groups in the short term; (2) strengthening non-profit and community housing providers in the medium term; and (3) embedding equity goals into provincial housing planning frameworks in the long term.

The key recommendation is a sequenced, coordinated, multi-level government strategy that implements these measures in an integrated, time-phased manner.

Primary audience: Ontario Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing (MMAH). Secondary stakeholders: Municipal housing divisions and non-profit housing providers.

Context & Evidence

Ontario's housing crisis is characterized by a significant supply gap, high rents, and unequal access to affordable housing. Ontario has the largest housing supply gap in Canada (CMHC, 2025a); easing affordability pressures in the Greater Toronto Area could require up to 70% more homebuilding. Rental prices are roughly three times higher than a decade ago and have significantly outpaced wage growth (Ontario Housing Affordability Task Force, 2022). Access is also highly unequal across population groups.

Indigenous people

More than 17% of Indigenous Canadians — many of whom live in Ontario — reside in overcrowded housing (Melvin & Anderson, 2022). Indigenous households in the province are disproportionately low-income and often spend more than 50% of their income on housing (CMHC, 2025b).

Newcomer immigrants

Newcomer families are significantly more likely than long-standing residents to spend over 30% of their income on shelter; by 2021, more than one-third of recent immigrants in Ontario were in unaffordable housing (Gorski, 2023).

Single mothers

Female-headed lone-parent households are substantially more likely than two-parent households to be in core housing need (Schwan et al., 2020) — a pattern strongly reflected in Ontario, where lone mothers represent a large demographic group.

Ontario's current policies focus on supply growth and the lowest-income households, largely missing people who are ineligible for social housing but priced out of the private market. The policy mix also insufficiently confronts discrimination in the rental market, and supply-focused reforms emphasize overall unit counts rather than the type, location, and tenure of housing these groups need.

Policy Options

Option 1 — Status quo

Ontario continues prioritizing supply expansion and preservation of existing social and affordable housing (COCHI, OPHI, HPP, COHB). Strongest at the system level, but continues to overlook mid-income households priced out of the private market and insufficiently addresses rental-market discrimination.

Option 2 — Short-term (0–2 years)

Targeted expansion of portable housing benefits for equity-priority groups. A time-limited expansion of portable benefits within COHB, targeting newcomers, off-reserve Indigenous households, and single-parent families who are severely rent-burdened but ineligible for social housing.

Objective: Reduce rent-burden among equity-priority households currently excluded from social housing.

Timeline: Year 1 — expand COHB eligibility and funding; Year 2 — pilot rent supplements in high-pressure markets (GTA, Hamilton, Waterloo).

Option 3 — Medium-term (2–5 years)

Expanded role for non-profit and community housing providers. Expand partnerships with non-profit and Indigenous housing providers to act as intermediaries between tenants and the private market — providing tenant screening, support services, and lease guarantees that lower barriers for marginalized groups.

Named partners: Ontario Aboriginal Housing Services, YWCA Toronto, COSTI Immigrant Services.

Instruments: Lease guarantees; damage mitigation funds; standardized tenancy support contracts.

Option 4 — Long-term (5+ years)

Embedding equity objectives into provincial housing planning frameworks. Formally integrate equity metrics into long-term planning — targets for unit size, proximity to transit and care infrastructure, and affordability relative to local incomes — adaptable to the impact of short- and medium-term interventions.

Recommendations

Primary recommendation. Ontario should prioritize short-term targeted affordability tools — particularly portable housing benefits and rent guarantees for equity-priority populations — while simultaneously embedding equity conditions into provincial funding and planning frameworks over the medium term. This addresses immediate access barriers without waiting for new supply to come online.

Secondary recommendation. The federal government should use Ontario's experience to refine National Housing Strategy instruments, encouraging provinces to adopt portable benefits, risk-sharing mechanisms, and equity-aligned planning criteria — rewarding improvements in access for marginalized sub-populations, not only increases in total supply.
