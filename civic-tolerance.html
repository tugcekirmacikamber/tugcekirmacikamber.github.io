Research Presentation · CAnD3 Dragon's Den

From Consensus to Divergence

Generational shifts in civic tolerance · United States, 1972–2024

This presentation, delivered as part of the CAnD3 Dragon's Den, uses the U.S. General Social Survey (1972–2024) to trace how civic tolerance has shifted across generations — from the Greatest Generation through Gen Z. It shows that while older cohorts grew more tolerant of communists, racists, and militarists alike, younger cohorts increasingly filter free-speech tolerance through the potential harm of that speech to marginalized communities — a move toward what I call "multicultural tolerance."

Reflection

How this connects to my doctoral research

My dissertation examines how shifts toward far right and authoritarian governance reshape the policing of dissent. This project approaches a related question from the demand side: how publics themselves come to draw the boundaries of acceptable speech and political expression. Understanding generational change in who deserves civil liberties helps explain the shifting terrain on which states justify — and publics tolerate — the regulation of protest.
